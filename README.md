# 🌧️ Assessing the Economic Impact of Flooding in Lodi, NJ: A Permeable Pavement Feasibility Study

**Montclair State University – EAES575, Environmental Economics**
*Ivan De la Paz and Karina Magro Machado*

---

## Project Overview

This project investigates the economic impact of recurrent flooding in Lodi, New Jersey, and evaluates the feasibility of permeable (porous) pavement as a long-term flood-mitigation strategy. The study combines ten years of Saddle River flood-gage data, a cost-benefit model for porous asphalt installation across 14 strategic sites, and an original 54-respondent door-to-door resident survey measuring flood impact and willingness to pay for green infrastructure.

The analysis finds that installing porous asphalt across roughly 10% of Lodi's flood-affected area (3.09M of 27.9M sq ft) could reduce long-term flood damages by more than $1.5 billion over ten years, far outweighing the estimated $37 million installation and maintenance costs. Survey findings reveal moderate community support, with most residents willing to contribute up to $100 annually, though many require evidence of proven success before committing. Overall, the project illustrates how targeted green infrastructure investments can significantly strengthen Lodi’s resilience while offering an economically viable pathway to long-term flood reduction.

## Tools & Methods

- **Flood Data:** USGS Saddle River gage-height records (2014–2024), converted to flood volume using affected-area and crest-level calculations
- **Economic Modeling:** Site-by-site cost-benefit analysis across 14 strategic installation locations, comparing porous asphalt installation cost against modeled flood-damage avoidance
- **Survey Design & Analysis:** Original 54-respondent resident survey (Qualtrics, door-to-door distribution) covering demographics, flood impact history, and willingness to pay
- **Tools Used:** Excel (flood volume modeling, cost-benefit calculations, survey data analysis)
